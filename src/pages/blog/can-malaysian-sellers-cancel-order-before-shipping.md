Every order. On video. Dispute-proof.

A seller sees something wrong with an order and wants to cancel before it ships. The first question is almost never legal — it is practical: *can I, without wrecking my shop?*

Here is the honest answer for Malaysian marketplace sellers: yes, you can cancel before shipping in certain situations, but every marketplace treats cancellation differently, and the way you cancel matters more than the fact of cancelling.

## When cancellation is clearly allowed

You are generally on firm ground when the order itself is broken:

- The buyer's delivery details cannot be completed by the courier.
- The address is outside the marketplace's supported delivery area.
- The item is out of stock and cannot be fulfilled.
- The buyer asks you to cancel (for COD orders, they may simply not answer the courier).

These are not judgement calls about the buyer — they are objective problems with fulfilment. Platforms expect you to cancel those.

## When cancellation gets dangerous

Cancellation becomes risky when it looks like you are refusing to serve a buyer. High cancellation rates hurt your metrics across every major Malaysian platform:

- Shopee tracks cancellation rate as part of seller health; repeated cancellations can limit your account.
- TikTok Shop uses order defect and fulfilment metrics that affect visibility and seller level.
- Lazada similarly weighs cancellations and late fulfilment in its performance score.

A pattern of cancelling orders that *look* risky — without a documented reason — reads as seller behaviour, not buyer behaviour. And some buyers report sellers who cancel for weak reasons, which adds another complaint to your record.

## The smarter question: verify before you cancel

Before you cancel, ask what you can verify:

1. **Is the reason objective?** Address incomplete, item unavailable, delivery impossible — document it.
2. **Can you reach the buyer?** A quick message often fixes a bad address. Many "risky" orders are just sloppy ones.
3. **Do you have a record?** If you ship, your packing video is your record. If you cancel, keep screenshots of the reason and the conversation.

## What protects you more than cancelling

Here is the uncomfortable truth: cancellation protects you from one order, but it does nothing for the orders you cannot see coming. The sellers who survive dispute-heavy periods are not the ones who cancel the most — they are the ones with the strongest records.

A packing video that shows the item going into the parcel, sealed and labelled, with a timestamp you can prove:

- Shortens "item not received" battles.
- Answers "the box arrived empty" with footage instead of words.
- Keeps you calm when a buyer's story changes mid-claim.

And because the record belongs to you (stored in your own cloud), you are never locked into a platform's archive to defend yourself.

## The difference between refusing and protecting

Marketplaces notice patterns, and so do buyers. If every cancellation you make happens right after a buyer asks a specific question, your shop starts to look like it is avoiding customers — and that reputation travels. The better frame is protection, not refusal: you protect your margin by verifying fulfilment problems, and you protect your standing by keeping cancellations rare, documented, and objective. When a genuine problem order appears, cancel it cleanly and move on; when a normal order merely makes you nervous, that nervousness is a signal to record better, not to refuse. Sellers who internalise this difference keep their cancellation rates low while still walking away from the orders that genuinely cannot be fulfilled — and they never have to explain a pattern of "gut feeling" cancellations to a platform officer.

## If you do cancel: keep the paper trail

Decide to cancel? Treat it like a mini case file:

- Screenshot the order page showing the reason (address error, out of stock, buyer request).
- Save the chat where you asked the buyer to clarify.
- Note the time and date of the cancellation.

This protects you twice: if the platform asks why you cancelled, you have an answer; and if the buyer later re-orders or leaves feedback, you have context. Most sellers who get burned by cancellations are not burned by the cancellation itself — they are burned by having no record of why they did it.
