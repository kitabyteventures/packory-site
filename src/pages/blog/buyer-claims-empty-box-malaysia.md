Every order. On video. Dispute-proof.

*"Encik, kotak sampai kosong. Barang tak ada dalam."* Few messages hit harder than this one. Your order shipped, the courier log shows delivery, and now the buyer says the parcel arrived empty. An **empty box claim** asks one simple question — did the item actually leave your hands? — and the answer decides a refund, a return, and possibly a formal dispute case.

How you respond in the first few hours matters as much as the evidence you can produce later. Here's what to do when a buyer claims the box arrived empty: which first reactions to avoid, what the platform will ask for, and how to assemble a reply that actually holds up.

## First reactions to avoid

When the claim lands, three instincts usually make things worse:

- **Don't accuse the buyer of lying.** Most empty-box reports are genuine confusion, courier mishandling, or theft somewhere in transit — not scams. Even if you suspect fraud, an accusatory reply escalates the conversation and makes you look defensive in front of the dispute team. Stay factual and helpful.
- **Don't refund immediately out of fear.** A quick refund closes the ticket, but it also settles the claim without any investigation. If the parcel was lost or opened by the courier, you're paying for a loss that isn't yours.
- **Don't reply empty-handed.** A message that says "we packed it properly, sir" carries no weight. Reply promptly — but reply with records.

## What the platform will ask for

Marketplaces run disputes on evidence. When a buyer claims an empty parcel, the resolution team typically asks you for:

- Order and tracking details
- What was packed, and when — photos, videos, or packing records
- How the parcel was sealed and packaged
- Courier scan and handover records
- Your chat history with the buyer

The exact requirements differ by marketplace and claim type, so **check the latest policy in your Seller Centre** before submitting. What stays constant is this: both sides get asked to show what happened, and the side with clearer evidence has the easier conversation.

## Why video evidence is your strongest reply to an empty box claim

An empty box claim is decided on one fact: was the item inside the parcel when it left you? A photo of a packed box doesn't prove that — anyone can pack a box, photograph it, and remove the item a minute later. What holds up is a **continuous packing video** that shows:

- The actual item, clearly visible on camera
- The item going into the box
- The box being sealed — tape over the flaps, intact
- The AWB barcode, tying the clip to the specific order
- A timestamp burned into the footage

That sequence gives one coherent answer: *this order left my hands with this item inside a sealed parcel.* It also quietly protects the buyer — if the parcel genuinely arrived empty, your video shows the courier received it intact, which points the investigation at the right link in the chain instead of at you.

## Assemble your evidence kit

If a dispute opens, don't scramble. Gather everything into one folder per order:

- The packing video, with AWB and timestamp visible
- AWB number and order ID
- The parcel weight recorded at packing, if you use a scale
- Courier handover scan or drop-off receipt
- Product photos or listing reference — plus serial or IMEI for electronics

Keep the kit ready before you need it: one folder per order, named by AWB number, saved somewhere you can reach from your phone. When you submit, present in order — video first, supporting records after.

## Prevention: record every order, not just the expensive ones

The empty box claims that hurt most come from orders you never expected to be questioned. If you only record RM500+ items, you leave a gap exactly where a claim can surface — and a seller who has proof for some orders but not others has a hard time explaining the difference.

Practical habits that cut claims at the source:

- Record every packing session, cheap items included — proof should be routine, not selective
- Seal with tape over the opening and write the parcel weight on the box
- Hand parcels to the courier yourself when you can, and keep the scan receipt
- Send buyers proof before the parcel ships — most questions never get asked
