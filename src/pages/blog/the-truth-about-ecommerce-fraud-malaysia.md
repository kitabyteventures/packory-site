## The silent cost of "small" claims

Malaysian e-commerce sellers know the pattern: order ships, courier confirms delivery, and then the message arrives — *"box sampai kosong"*, *"saya tak terima barang"*, or a parcel-swap accusation. The product value is only part of the loss. You also lose the refund, the dispute time, and a slice of trust with every claim you can't prove.

Screenshots don't settle these disputes. Timestamps can be argued, chat logs can be cropped, and a photo of a packed box doesn't show what actually left your hands.

## Why video evidence changes the equation

A continuous, timestamped video of the order being packed creates a record that is hard to argue with:

- **What was packed** — the actual item, not a photo staged after the fact
- **When it was packed** — a burned-in timestamp ties the clip to the order moment
- **That it left intact** — seal and packaging condition visible before handover
- **Who can verify it** — an evidence link the buyer (and the dispute team) can open

This isn't surveillance. It's a proof layer that protects honest sellers and honest buyers alike — the difference between *he-said-she-said* and *here's the video*.

## The claims that actually hurt sellers

Most sellers we talk to in Malaysian marketplace communities describe the same handful of dispute patterns:

1. **Empty-box claims** — buyer says the box arrived empty, item missing.
2. **Parcel-swap claims** — buyer says the parcel they received wasn't what they ordered (and returns something else).
3. **Item-not-received** — buyer says delivery never happened, despite courier confirmation.
4. **Wrong-item / damaged-on-arrival** — condition disputes that swing on packaging proof.

What all four have in common: they're decided on evidence. Sellers who can produce a clean, timestamped packing record start from a much stronger position than sellers who can only offer screenshots and assurances.
