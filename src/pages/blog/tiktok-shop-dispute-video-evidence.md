TikTok Shop disputes are part of running a live-selling business. Volume moves fast, and with volume comes the occasional claim: the parcel arrived with the wrong item, the box showed up empty, or the buyer insists they never received what the courier's photo shows delivered. The moment a case opens, sellers want two answers: how long is this going to take, and what actually wins it?

The first answer, honestly, is that timelines vary. The second answer is more useful: disputes are decided on evidence, and timestamped packing video is the strongest evidence a seller can submit. This post walks through the general shape of a TikTok Shop dispute, why video evidence carries weight, and how to record it without slowing down your packing line.

## How long do TikTok Shop disputes take?

There is no single number, and anyone quoting one is guessing. Resolution time depends on the case type, how quickly both sides respond, the quality of the evidence, and TikTok Shop's current policy — which is updated regularly. The reliable move is to open your Seller Centre and read the dispute section as it stands today, because response windows and review timelines do change.

That said, the general shape of a TikTok Shop dispute is consistent:

1. **The buyer opens a case** — usually item not received, item not as described, or damage or empty parcel.
2. **The seller gets a window to respond** with evidence.
3. **Both sides may add messages and evidence**, and the case can go back and forth.
4. **TikTok Shop reviews the file** and issues a decision; depending on the case, an appeal step may follow.

What lengthens disputes in practice is rarely the platform. It's sellers who respond late, submit weak evidence, or get pulled into long back-and-forth chats. Cases move faster when the first submission is complete: respond inside the window, attach clean evidence, and let it speak.

## What actually decides the outcome

Platforms don't decide TikTok Shop disputes on impressions. Reviewers work from whatever record both sides can produce:

- **Courier and delivery records** — tracking events and delivery confirmation from the courier side
- **Messages between buyer and seller** — what was promised, what was claimed, and when
- **Photos and videos** — anything showing the order's condition at packing or at delivery
- **Order details** — the listing, the variant, and the value at stake

You have limited control over most of those categories. You can't rewrite what the courier logged. But the packing record is the one piece of the puzzle that is fully yours to create — and it's the piece that shows what actually left your hands.

## Why packing video evidence wins TikTok Shop disputes

A video of the order being packed — item visible, packaging sealed, a timestamp burned into the frame — answers the questions dispute reviewers actually ask:

- **What was packed?** The item appears in the frame, not in a photo staged later.
- **When was it packed?** A UTC timestamp ties the clip to the order moment.
- **Was it intact when it left?** The seal and packaging condition are visible before handover.

Compare that with the evidence most sellers submit: chat screenshots, a photo of a closed box, an order status page. All of it can be argued. A continuous, timestamped clip is a record, not a claim. In a he-said-she-said, "here's the video" changes the conversation.

This isn't about fear — jual proof, bukan fear. It's about making proof so routine that it costs you nothing and protects every order, not just the ones you're worried about.

## The moment a dispute opens, do this

1. **Check the Seller Centre first.** Read the current timeline and your response window before anything else — policies update, and yesterday's numbers may not be today's.
2. **Respond inside the window.** A late response is the easiest way to lose a case you could have won.
