Every order. On video. Dispute-proof.

Sellers across Malaysia started September with a new notice in their Seller Centre: a Shopee shipping fee claim service that automates refunds of courier costs for RM0.20 per order. It sounds like a small number, and for many shops the reaction is to sign up quickly and move on. The more useful question is what the service actually covers — because fee claims and packing disputes are not the same game, and the one that threatens your margin is the one automation does not touch.

## What the Shopee shipping fee claim service covers

The service — often shortened to SSFS — handles a specific job: when a parcel is lost or delayed and you are entitled to recover the shipping fee you paid, the claim is submitted and followed up for you. Instead of tracking web forms and deadlines yourself, you pay RM0.20 per order and the recovery is automated. For sellers who ship enough volume that courier fee claims happen regularly, the arithmetic can genuinely work in their favour.

Two things are worth noting before relying on it. First, terms, coverage windows, and claim conditions are set by Shopee Malaysia and do change — check the current policy in your Seller Centre rather than acting on a summary. Second, notice what the service is recovering: a fee. Money you paid to move the parcel, returned when the movement fails. It is not deciding who was at fault, and it is not covering the value of the goods.

## The disputes RM0.20 per order does not cover

This is where the gap shows up. Across Malaysian marketplace communities, sellers report seeing the same patterns again and again:

- **Empty-box claims** — the buyer says the parcel arrived with the box empty: *barang sampai kosong*.
- **Parcel-swap claims** — the buyer says what arrived was not what they ordered.
- **Item-not-received claims** — the buyer says delivery never happened, even when courier records say otherwise.
- **Wrong-item or damaged claims** — condition disputes that turn on what you actually packed and sealed.

None of these are fee claims. Each one is a dispute about facts: what was inside that specific parcel, in what condition, at the moment it left your hands. The automated service can recover your shipping cost, but it cannot answer the question "what did you actually pack?" — because nobody from the platform stood at your packing table. The only record of that moment is the one you keep.

## What a lost dispute costs on thin margins

Fee automation saves you small amounts across many orders. A packing dispute you lose costs you the whole item, the shipping, the refund, and the hours spent on the case — and on the tight margins most Malaysian sellers operate on, that single loss can erase the profit from a long run of orders. The asymmetry is why evidence matters more than the fee automation trend suggests. It is not about being suspicious of buyers. It is about making sure the facts of your side of the parcel are never in question when a claim arrives.

## Why video evidence settles what fee claims cannot

Marketplace reviewers decide factual disputes on evidence. They want plain answers: what was in the parcel, what condition it was in, and when it was packed and handed over. A continuous video of the packing moment answers all three in one record — showing the item go in, the box seal, and the AWB on screen with a trustworthy time reference. Evidence like that does not argue with the buyer's story; it simply makes the story unnecessary to rely on.

The practical challenge has always been consistency. Recording every order by hand is easy to skip on a busy day. That is why sellers who treat evidence as an operating habit use a hands-free workflow: scan the AWB barcode and the recording starts automatically, a UTC timestamp is burned into every frame, and a thumbs-up stops the clip. The video syncs to your own Google Drive, so the record stays in your control, and an evidence link is sent to the buyer on WhatsApp before the parcel ships — dated, and sitting in the chat from day one if a claim ever comes later.
