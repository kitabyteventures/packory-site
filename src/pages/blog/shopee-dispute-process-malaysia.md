No seller searches for "shopee dispute process" out of curiosity. The search usually happens the day a return request stops being a chat thread and becomes something more formal — a buyer says the box arrived empty, a parcel-swap accusation appears, or courier records show delivery while the buyer insists nothing arrived. From that point, the conversation is a case, and both sides are expected to submit evidence. How it ends depends less on who is right and more on who can prove it.

## What the Shopee dispute process in Malaysia actually involves

At a high level, the flow is consistent. A buyer opens a return or refund request, and if you accept it, the case usually ends there. If you decline, or if the two sides cannot agree, the buyer can ask Shopee's team to review the case — that review is what sellers mean when they talk about a dispute. The team weighs the order record, tracking and delivery information, your replies, and the evidence both parties attach.

Two things are worth knowing before a dispute ever arrives. First, the process is evidence-based by design: a third party weighs what actually happened instead of taking either side's word. Second, the specific steps, timelines, and decision windows are set by Shopee Malaysia and do change over time — before any deadline matters, open your Seller Centre and read the current policy there. Treat the Seller Centre as the source of truth for the live process rather than relying on a blog post's summary of it.

## The claim types that reach disputes

Across Malaysian marketplace communities, the cases that actually reach review cluster into a handful of patterns:

- **Empty-box claims** — the buyer says the parcel arrived with the box empty: *barang sampai kosong*. Your only counter is proof of what went into that box.
- **Parcel-swap claims** — the buyer says the parcel they received was not what they ordered, and sometimes returns a different item entirely.
- **Item-not-received claims** — the buyer says delivery never happened, even though courier records show a completed delivery.
- **Damaged or wrong-item claims** — condition disputes where the argument often turns on how the item was packed and sealed at dispatch.

What all four share: each one is decided on evidence about the parcel at a specific moment — what it contained, in what condition, and when it left your hands. That moment is the one part of the journey you can document completely.

## What the review expects from your evidence

Reviewers are not looking for a dramatic story. They are looking for plain answers to three questions: What was in the parcel? What condition was it in when it left you? When was it packed and handed over? Evidence that answers those questions cleanly and attaches to the order is what moves a case.

In practice, usable evidence is:

- Tied to the order number and AWB, not generic
- Shows the item, the packaging, and the seal
- Carries a trustworthy time reference
- Is continuous enough to show the item going in and the box being sealed

Evidence that arrives late, in pieces, or without a clear link to the order makes a case harder to follow — and an easy case to lose on presentation rather than on facts.

## Why video evidence beats screenshots

A photo of a packed box answers only one question, and weakly: it shows a box that was packed at *some* point. It cannot show the item going in, and a screenshot can be taken any day after the fact. Photo timestamps get argued too — camera clocks drift, files get re-saved, metadata gets stripped.

A packing video is a different class of record. One continuous clip shows the item entering the box, the packaging closing, and the seal going on, with a time reference burned into the frame itself. It is hard to stage, hard to crop, and hard to re-date. When a reviewer is deciding between two versions of events — the buyer's and yours — a record like that is exactly the kind of evidence that settles the question instead of leaving it open.

## Practical steps when a dispute hits

If a case does land in your Seller Centre, the sequence below keeps you on solid ground:

1. **Respond early, not late.** Reviews run on windows, and silence reads as a weak case. The exact window is set per case — check the case page and your Seller Centre rather than guessing.
2. **Build one clean evidence file per case.** Order ID, AWB number, delivery record, chat log, and your packing evidence in a single place. Attach it all in your first response; drip-feeding evidence later makes a case look disorganised.
3. **Stay factual and calm.** Describe what you can prove — what you packed, when, and how it was handed over. Emotion persuades nobody on a review team.
4. **Never invent what you can't show.** If you did not record that order, state what you can prove and stop there. A shaky extra claim can damage an otherwise solid case.
5. **Use the in-platform channels.** Dispute communication and appeals run through the Seller Centre case flow — that is also where the current policy for your situation is published.
