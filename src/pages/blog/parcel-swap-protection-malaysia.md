Every order. On video. Dispute-proof.

*"Seller, parcel saya tukar. Saya terima barang orang lain."* If you sell online in Malaysia long enough, you will hear some variation of the parcel swap story. A **parcel swap claim** — the buyer says the parcel they received was not what you sent — sits among the trickiest dispute categories for e-commerce sellers, because it touches every link of the journey: packing, courier, and delivery.

Sellers report seeing these claims surface regularly in Malaysian marketplace communities: the wrong item inside the parcel, the empty parcel, the return that comes back as a different product. Without a record of what you actually packed, a parcel swap claim becomes your word against the buyer's — and screenshots don't change that.

## What sellers mean by "parcel swap"

The label covers a few related scenarios, and it helps to know which one you're facing:

- **Wrong-content claim** — the parcel arrives containing an item different from what was ordered: a genuine courier mix-up between parcels, or a claim made on a parcel that was packed correctly.
- **Empty parcel claim** — the parcel arrives with packaging intact but nothing inside. Often blamed on transit handling, but the evidence question is the same: what was in the parcel when it left the seller?
- **Return swap** — the buyer returns a different item, a used unit, or an empty box, claiming it is what they received.

Different causes, but they share one thing: the dispute is decided on evidence about what left your hands and in what condition.

## Why screenshots fail

Most sellers respond to these claims with screenshots — a photo of the packed box, a chat log, a packing slip. It feels like proof, but in a dispute it rarely holds:

- A photo of a sealed box shows the box, not the contents. Nothing in the image proves the item was inside.
- A packing photo can be staged after the fact, and phone timestamps can be argued.
- Chat screenshots only show what was said — they don't show what was shipped.
- A weight slip on its own doesn't connect to the specific item or the specific moment.

Static images show one moment, not a continuous truth. When both sides arrive with screenshots, the case drifts into he-said-she-said — and the resolution team has to guess. That is not where you want your refund decided.

## The evidence chain: pack → handover → delivery

Every parcel travels through three links, and a dispute is only as strong as the weakest one:

1. **Pack** — what went into the parcel, when, and in what condition. This is the link only you can prove.
2. **Handover** — the courier's scan and weight record when the parcel entered their network. This proves the parcel was received intact.
3. **Delivery** — the courier's proof of delivery at the buyer's address.

You cannot control what happens inside the courier network or at the buyer's door. But you can cover your own links completely, so that a later claim points the investigation at the right place instead of at you. A seller who can show an intact, sealed parcel with the item inside, handed to the courier under scan, has done everything on their side of the chain.

## Timestamped video as the anchor

The pack link is where most claims are won or lost, and it's the link a video anchors best. A **continuous, timestamped packing video** — not a photo, not an edited clip — connects the order to the parcel in one unbroken record:

- The AWB barcode appears at the start, tying the footage to the specific order
- The item and its accessories are visible before sealing
- The UTC timestamp is burned into every frame, so the clip cannot be backdated
- The seal is shown intact at the moment of handover

That single clip answers the question a parcel swap dispute is really asking: *was this item in this parcel when it left the seller?* And when the buyer receives the evidence link on WhatsApp before the parcel even ships, the conversation usually changes before a claim is filed.

## Practical protection checklist

Whether or not you use automation, these habits keep every order covered:

- Film every packing session — one continuous clip per order, AWB in frame
- Show the item, accessories, and any serial number before sealing
- Weigh the sealed parcel and note the figure with the AWB record
- Seal with tape over the flaps and mark the box with your shop name or the weight
- Hand the parcel to the courier and keep the scan or collection receipt
- Store videos in your own cloud — not only on the packer's phone
- Send the buyer the proof link before dispatch
- For returns, record the unboxing of the returned parcel before opening it, so a swap on the way back is covered too — and check your marketplace's return rules in the latest Seller Centre policy

This is the same discipline as keeping receipts — you only miss the record when you need it most.
