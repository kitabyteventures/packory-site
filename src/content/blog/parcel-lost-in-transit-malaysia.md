## The two shapes of a parcel lost in transit

**No movement at all.** The parcel was scanned once or twice, then nothing. It is stuck in a sorting centre, loaded onto the wrong van, or sitting in a branch nobody has checked.

**Delivered, then denied.** Tracking says delivered and the buyer says nothing arrived. That is not always dishonest. The parcel can be at the guard house, with a neighbour, in the office mailroom, or left at the gate.

Which shape you have tells you who to ask. The first is a courier trace. The second is an address conversation.

## The first 24 hours: what to check, in order

1. Open the full tracking history, not the status headline. Read the last scan, its location and its timestamp.
2. Ask the buyer to check the usual holding spots: guard house, neighbours, family at home, the office mailroom, and any self collection point.
3. Contact the courier with the AWB number and ask for a trace. Get the case reference.
4. Write the timeline down: order date, handover time, last scan, the day you reported it. You will reuse it for everyone involved.

Speed matters, because these processes run on deadlines.

## Who decides the claim, and what they ask for

- **The courier.** They work from the AWB, the handover scan and the weight. Compensation limits come from the courier's terms of service for the service you used, so read it rather than assume your goods' value is the ceiling.
- **The marketplace.** If you shipped on platform logistics, the case runs through their process. Steps and windows change, so check the current policy in your Seller Centre.
- **Declared value cover.** If you paid for a declaration on the parcel, that slip matters most.

Recovery is slow. A trace can take days and some parcels turn up late, so say that plainly instead of promising what you cannot control.

## Evidence that decides a lost parcel case

A tracking number proves a parcel moved. It does not prove what you handed over, in what condition, or that the right item was inside.

- The handover scan or drop off receipt, with the date and time visible.
- The weight, if the courier receipt prints it.
- A packing clip tied to the AWB, with the item, the seal and the label in one continuous video.
- The chat trail with the buyer, kept inside the platform.
- The case reference from the courier and from the platform.

## What to tell the buyer while the trace runs

Reply quickly, stay factual, do not accuse anyone. Most loss reports are genuine, and parcels can vanish in a sorting centre. Give the buyer the case reference and a realistic date for the next update, then update on that date even if nothing has moved. Seller silence is what turns a lost parcel into a bad review. If you refund or resend, decide from your own terms and margin, not from pressure. ## How to keep the next parcel from going quiet

- Hand over at the counter and watch the scan register. A drop box gives no scan and no weight.
- Apply the label flat and away from the seal, and check the address against the order as you stick it.
- Consider declared value cover on high value orders, and know the cost before you need it.
- Record every order, not only the risky ones. Parcels rarely vanish on the order you were suspicious about.
- Watch your own pattern. Losses clustering on one courier or drop off point are worth raising, with records attached.
- Check unfamiliar buyers before shipping a high value order. A quick [buyer check](/check-buyer/) takes a minute.

A lost parcel is a process problem more often than a fraud problem. Treat it that way and you spend the week sending documents, not arguing.
