You are not being asked to argue. You are being asked to show.

## Shopee return request: what lands in your Seller Centre

A return request is a case, not a chat message. It arrives with the order number, the reason the buyer selected, whatever the buyer uploaded, and a response window counted in hours rather than days. Open it from the order detail page, not from your notifications, so the whole file is in front of you.

Two things get decided inside that window: whether the return is accepted, and who carries the cost. Both lean on evidence, and the evidence you already hold beats the evidence you scramble for later.

## The four families of return reason

Reason lists change, so read the exact label on your own case before you reply. In practice the reasons fall into a few families.

- **Not received.** The buyer says the parcel never arrived, or arrived with contents missing. Courier scans, weight, and the handover receipt do the talking.
- **Damaged or defective.** The item arrived broken, leaking, or not working. How you packed it, and its condition on handover, matter most.
- **Not as described.** Wrong size, wrong colour, wrong model, or a listing photo promising something the item does not do.
- **Changed mind.** The buyer no longer wants it. Many marketplaces allow this inside a stated period, with conditions that differ by category, so check your Seller Centre.

The family decides what you attach. A not received case does not care about your bubble wrap. A damaged case does not care about your tracking number.

## What the reviewer looks at

Reviewers move through a file quickly, looking for a record that lines up end to end.

- A continuous clip of the item going into the box and the box being sealed, with the AWB visible in the same frame.
- The weight on record, taken when you packed, plus the courier handover scan.
- The chat history with the buyer, kept factual. Never insult a buyer in writing. That message becomes part of the case.
- Photographs of the pack if you have them, though a clip is harder to argue with than a still.

A tracking screenshot proves a parcel moved. It does not prove what was inside it, and that is the gap most sellers find after the request lands.

## When the parcel comes back different

This is where returns and empty box claims meet. The buyer returns an item, you open the parcel, and inside sits a different model, a used unit, or nothing at all. Sellers report seeing this pattern, and it is hard to win on description alone.

Record the return too. Film the sealed parcel before you open it, keep the courier label in frame, then open it on camera in one take. Keep both clips attached to the order, not floating in a gallery, because you may need them a month apart.

## Who carries the return shipping fee

Platforms decide this from the reason and the fault, and the rule moves more often than most sellers expect. Read the fee line on your case before you agree to anything, because accepting a return and accepting the fee are two separate clicks.

Where the buyer or the courier is at fault, marketplaces usually run a claim path so the seller is not left holding the cost, with its own deadline and its own paperwork. ## Your ten minute response checklist

- Read the exact reason and the deadline on the case.
- Pull the order from your recorder or your own storage by order number, not by scrolling dates.
- Attach the packing clip, the weight, and the handover scan.
- Write a short, calm reply: what you shipped, when, and what the record shows. No accusations.
- Check the fee line before you accept or contest anything.
- Diary the return parcel arrival, and record the unboxing when it lands.
